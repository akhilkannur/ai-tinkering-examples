# Content Strategy Notes

Notes from the July 2026 discussion about the future of Real AI Examples. These are observations to revisit, not a committed plan.

## Core diagnosis

A general AI examples directory has a weak reason to exist on its own. Social feeds, newsletters, communities, and AI assistants already provide discovery. More listings, badges, or design improvements would not fundamentally change that.

The site currently emphasizes its least defensible layer: finding things that other people have done with AI.

## What Marketing Examples actually does

Marketing Examples is not valuable merely because it collects examples. Harry Dry:

- Serves a recognizable audience of marketers and founders.
- Curates the original artifact but creates the observation.
- Distills each example into a short, reusable lesson.
- Adds judgment about why it works and how to apply it.
- Uses a distinctive visual format and his own personality for distribution.

The key difference is not article length. Harry's lessons are also short.

> Marketing Examples: "Here is something I noticed about marketing."
>
> Real AI Examples today: "Here is something someone did with AI."

## A possible direction

One possible positioning is:

> Practical AI workflow lessons for lean marketing teams. Short breakdowns of workflows worth copying.

This would require:

1. Selecting examples for one audience rather than publishing miscellaneous AI uses.
2. Turning each description into an original, reusable observation.
3. Making each post useful without requiring the reader to open the source.
4. Making the editor's judgment and identity more visible.
5. Publishing fewer, stronger lessons rather than maintaining listing volume.

The internal Processor, Researcher, and Hybrid archetypes may help structure workflows, but users should encounter recognizable jobs and outcomes instead of internal classifications.

## Curation versus original production

The site does not need to generate every example from scratch.

The model can remain:

> Curate the workflow, create the observation.

For each example:

1. Find a real workflow or result from a creator.
2. Use the artifact with attribution or permission.
3. Identify the non-obvious, transferable lesson.
4. Turn that lesson into a concise visual breakdown.
5. Link to the original creator as the source.

When a source lacks enough detail or evidence, the options are to ask the creator, reproduce part of the workflow, or skip it. Reproduction is not mandatory for every post, but unsupported claims should not be presented as proven.

## Social distribution challenge

AI workflows are invisible plumbing and are less naturally shareable than great copy. The social post should lead with the visible or surprising result, not a workflow diagram.

A useful formula is:

> Specific task + visible artifact + surprising observation + one transferable lesson

Possible social formats:

- Before-and-after outputs.
- Bad AI result versus corrected result.
- An annotated screenshot of the finished deliverable.
- A comparison of several methods or models.
- Manual time versus AI-assisted time.
- A short screen recording of the workflow.
- A real template produced by the workflow.

Example:

> I asked three AI models to turn one customer call into ad copy. All three invented customer language. The best workflow generated the ads only after extracting verbatim quotes. Here is the difference.

This can become a short carousel showing the transcript, generic output, quote-backed output, and the instruction that produced the improvement.

Workflows with visual outputs are therefore more suitable for social distribution: ads, landing pages, research reports, competitor comparisons, content briefs, emails, and customer-insight tables. Invisible back-office automations may be useful but are harder to distribute organically.

## Important caveat

Narrowing the audience and rewriting snippets will not automatically create traction. If existing entries already contain original, independently useful observations, the larger problem is more likely distribution, personality, or insufficient proof.

The strategic question to revisit is whether the project should become an editor-led teaching product for a specific audience, rather than continue primarily as a broad database.
